Executive Summary

EduNode is a plug-and-play school intelligence platform consisting of a pre-configured local server device ("the Node") and a cloud-based SaaS dashboard. Schools in underserved and low-connectivity areas install one Node per campus. Students and teachers connect to it via Wi-Fi on any device — phone, tablet, or laptop — and access a full curriculum library, assessment engine, and teacher tools without needing the internet.

When the school's internet is available — even briefly — the Node syncs usage data, student performance metrics, and content updates to EduNode's cloud dashboard, giving school admins, LGA officers, and state ministries real-time visibility into learning outcomes across every connected school.

Problem & Market

The Connectivity Crisis in Nigerian Schools

Nigeria has 109,000 public schools. As of early 2025, UNICEF's GenU 9JA programme — a major public-private initiative involving Airtel, ATC Nigeria, IHS, and the Federal Ministry of Education — had connected just 1,027 of them to the internet. That is less than 1%. The remaining 108,000+ schools operate entirely offline.

Even where connectivity exists, the economics are brutal. The median price of a 2GB mobile broadband plan in Nigeria costs 4.2% of per capita GNI — more than double the UN Broadband Commission's affordability threshold and the highest of any ITU region globally. For public school administrators, paying for sustained school-wide internet access is simply not feasible.

Device Scarcity In southern public junior secondary schools, device ratios range from 37 to 61 students per device. In northern states, the ratios are even worse. Yet students increasingly have personal smartphones — meaning the school network, not the device, is the missing link.

Beachhead: Nigerian Public Secondary Schools

EduNode will focus initial commercial effort on Nigerian public secondary schools (JSS1–SS3) in states with active WAEC/NECO intervention programmes. These schools have the most acute need (WAEC pass rates below 40% in many LGAs), a motivated buyer (state ministries seeking data), and an established procurement pathway (SUBEB and state MoE budgets).

How It Works — Data Flow

Step-by-step flow 1. School installs Node, connects to school router → students see "EduNode Wi-Fi" or access edu.local on any browser
2. Students browse curriculum content, watch videos, take past-question quizzes — all stored locally
3. All interactions (sessions, quiz scores, subject access) are logged to the local SQLite database on the Node
4. When any internet is detected (school's WAN, a visiting staff member's hotspot, etc.), Node syncs a compressed analytics payload to EduNode cloud via HTTPS
5. School admin, LGA, and ministry users see live dashboards on cloud.edunode.ng
6. New curriculum content, software updates, and AI-generated quizzes are pushed to the Node during sync windows

Local App — Student-Facing Features

  • Curriculum Library: NERDC-aligned video lessons for JSS1–SS3. All subjects. Chapters broken into 8–15 minute segments.
  • Past Question Bank: WAEC, NECO, JAMB past questions from 2005–present. Subject-filtered, year-filtered, timed mock exams.
  • AI Quiz Generator: (when Node has occasional internet) Uses Claude API to generate new practice questions on any topic.
  • Offline Textbooks: NERDC-approved digital textbooks in PDF/ePub for every subject and class level.
  • Teacher Tools: Lesson planning assistant, class result entry, attendance marking, printable worksheets.
  • Notice Board: School admin posts announcements; students see them on login screen.

Cloud Dashboard — Admin & Government Features

  • School Overview: Active students, sessions per week, top subjects, assessment scores.
  • LGA Roll-up: Aggregate performance across all EduNode schools in an LGA. Identify struggling schools.
  • State Ministry View: State-level reporting. Export CSV/PDF reports for SUBEB submissions.
  • Content Management: Upload new curriculum packages, push to all Nodes in a region.
  • Device Health: Node uptime, storage capacity, last sync timestamp per school.
  • Billing & Licensing: Manage subscriptions, renewals, and payment history per school.

Technology Stack

Node Software (Local Server)
Raspberry Pi OSLite (headless) Debian-based OS. Minimal footprint, stable, proven in field deployments across Africa.
Node.js + FastifyServes the local web app at edu.local. Lightweight, fast, familiar to your existing stack.
SQLite + LitestreamLocal database. Litestream streams WAL to S3 when internet available. Same stack as learn2earnhq.
Next.js (PWA)Student-facing web app served locally. Works in browser, installable as PWA. No app store needed.
FFmpegServes video content efficiently. Supports adaptive bitrate for slow Wi-Fi connections.
hostapd + dnsmasqCreates the local Wi-Fi hotspot and routes edu.local DNS to the Node IP address.
Cloud Platform
Next.js 14Admin dashboard SaaS. Server components for fast initial load. App router for clean navigation.
PostgreSQLCloud database for schools, subscriptions, sync payloads, and aggregated analytics.
AWS EC2 + S3EC2 for API server, S3 for content distribution and Litestream backup. Already in your infrastructure.
NginxReverse proxy, virtual hosting for cloud.edunode.ng. Same setup as ResultsPRO.
PM2Process management for cloud Node.js server. Same pattern as existing deployments.
PaystackNigerian payment processing for school subscriptions. Supports card, bank transfer, and USSD payment.
AI & Content
Anthropic Claude APIAI quiz generation, content summarisation, and teacher lesson plan assistance when sync window is open.
ffmpeg + yt-dlpContent pipeline tool for ingesting and packaging curriculum video content into Node-compatible format.
Custom CMSInternal tool to manage curriculum packages, tag content by subject/class/term, and push to Node fleets.
Hardware & DevOps
Raspberry Pi ImagerCustom OS image with pre-configured software. Flash-once-and-ship manufacturing workflow.
AnsibleRemote configuration management for Node fleet. Push config changes over SSH when internet is available.
SentryError monitoring on both cloud and Node software. Batches errors and syncs when connected.
TailscaleZero-config VPN for secure remote access into Nodes for debugging and support without exposing ports.

Go-To-Market Strategy

PHASE 01
Network-Led Pilot
Use personal network to get 5 school heads to accept free 3-month pilot. Measure engagement, get testimonials. This is the proof of concept that unlocks everything else. Target private secondary schools in Lagos and Ogun first — faster decision-making than government schools.
PHASE 02
Field Sales & Grants
Hire one Lagos-based field sales agent who visits schools in person. This is a relationship-driven sale. Simultaneously apply for every edtech grant available — Giga, Tony Elumelu, Shuttleworth, USAID, GIZ. Grant capital subsidizes commercial traction.
PHASE 03
Government Entry Point
ResultsPRO gives you an existing relationship with school administrators. Use that as a warm intro. Target state Universal Basic Education Board (SUBEB) and WAEC intervention programmes as institutional buyers. One state contract can mean 50–200 schools at once.

Competitive Landscape

RACHEL (World Possible)
NGO / Nonprofit
Generic global content (Khan Academy, Wikipedia). No Nigeria curriculum alignment. No SaaS dashboard. No commercial model. No analytics. Deployed via charities — cannot scale commercially. EduNode wins on: local content, data layer, business sustainability.
Kolibri (Learning Equality)
NGO / Open Source
Excellent offline LMS but entirely donor-funded. No WAEC/NECO alignment. No hardware product. Implemented by NGOs, not commercially. No recurring revenue model for sustainability at scale in Nigeria. EduNode wins on: commercial viability, local curriculum, hardware simplicity.
ULesson / PrepClass
Nigerian Edtech (Online)
Well-funded Nigerian online platforms with excellent curriculum content but require reliable internet and student data subscriptions. Exclude the 100,000+ schools without connectivity. EduNode wins on: offline-first, schools as buyers (not students), institutional play.
The Real Gap No commercially sustainable, Nigeria-curriculum-aligned, offline-first, school-intelligence platform exists. RACHEL and Kolibri serve the access problem but have no business model. Ulesson and PrepClass serve connected students but miss the schools entirely. EduNode is the first product that sits at the intersection of all three needs.

EduNode's Moat

Nigeria Curriculum Lock-In
NERDC-aligned content is a deep moat. Licensing, curating, and tagging 6 years of curriculum content is a 12–18 month effort that competitors cannot easily replicate. Schools don't switch when content is embedded.
Hardware + Software Bundle
Schools don't want to manage separate hardware and software vendors. A single responsible party for the whole system (device + content + dashboard) is a strong retention mechanism.
Government Data Relationships
Once state ministries are viewing EduNode dashboards for school performance, replacing the platform means losing institutional data continuity. Switching costs grow rapidly with deployment scale.
Fleet Effect
Every additional Node deployed improves content recommendations, AI quiz generation quality, and benchmark comparisons for all schools. Network value increases with scale, creating a virtuous cycle.

Risks & Mitigation

RiskSeverityLikelihoodMitigation
Hardware theft / damage — Schools in low-security environments may lose Nodes High High Tamper-evident enclosure, remote disable via cloud (kills auth token on sync), hardware insurance option, school admin accountability clause in licence agreement
Content licensing cost — Publishers or NERDC demand high fees for curriculum content High Med Start with openly licensed content (NTI, Khan Academy localized). Commission original content. Negotiate government access for public school use. Build in-house content team early.
Slow government procurement — State ministry deals take 12–24 months to close Med High Private school commercial sales sustain cash flow while government deals develop. Never rely on a single government deal in any quarter. Build pipeline of 10x the needed contracts.
Naira devaluation — Hardware priced in USD, revenue in NGN High Med Fix hardware price in USD equivalent on contracts. Source locally assembled alternatives as local electronics manufacturing matures. Build NGN currency buffer on hardware orders.
Cheap Android tablet competition — Government imports cheap preloaded tablets Med Med EduNode is the software/data layer, not just a device. Position as platform, not device. Support running EduNode software on government-procured tablets as an alternative SKU.
Low school payment culture — Schools agree then fail to pay on renewal Med Med Annual payment upfront (not monthly). Content library is locked/degraded on expired licence. Government and NGO schools are fully pre-funded before deployment. Payment via Paystack bank transfer (schools have accounts).
Rapid connectivity expansion — Starlink / Giga connects all schools faster than expected Low Low EduNode's value extends beyond offline access — the curriculum library, AI tools, and government analytics layer remain valuable even with internet. Platform evolves to hybrid online/offline.
